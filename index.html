TARLAC
PROVINCE

Tarlac is a landlocked province located in the Central Luzon region of the Philippines. Because it is landlocked, it does not have a coastline. It is shaped somewhat like a triangle and is bordered by Pangasinan to the north, Nueva Ecija to the east, Zambales to the west, and Pampanga to the south.
The province covers a total land area of approximately 3,053 square kilometers. Its terrain is a mix of vast, flat plains in the eastern part (making up about 75% of the province), which are ideal for agriculture, and rugged, hilly, or mountainous areas in the west. A portion of the famous Mount Pinatubo is also located within the province.

04 — The Province At A Glance

Discover Tarlac

Often referred to as the "Melting Pot of Central Luzon," Tarlac is known for its diverse population. The province is home to a mix of several ethnic groups, primarily the Kapampangans in the south, Ilocanos and Pangasinenses in the north, and Tagalogs near the border with Nueva Ecija.
